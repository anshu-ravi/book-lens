Who is Kell, and why does Ansa keep him at arm's length?

Kell runs the tally house at the foot of the causeway, which in a town that lives off the tide makes him the closest thing the salt trade has to a magistrate."Kell kept the numbers for everyone and traded for no one…" He is not a merchant himself; he keeps other people's numbers, and he keeps them well enough that nobody has managed to prove he keeps two sets.

Ansa's wariness reads less like dislike than like caution around someone whose interests she cannot locate. Their first real exchange is entirely about the weight of a shipment, and she notices that he corrects her arithmetic without once looking at the ledger.Ask about this"Eleven short," he said, before the ledger was open… After that she is careful to tell him true things and incomplete ones.

What she actually suspects hasn't been said out loud yet — the text keeps her reasoning to herself, and by chapter twenty-two it is still something she is testing rather than something she has decided."She had a shape for it by then but no name…"
